Allocation Agent

Money lands in a bank account. Somewhere in the books there is a record of what it was for. This connects them — and when one payment covers several records, it notices that instead of guessing.

2What happens to one record

Every record walks these four steps in order. After a run, the counts below each step show how many records left the pipeline there.

step 1
Narrow
Look up ledger entries sharing this account, within a week of this date. Cuts the search from every entry in the book to a few dozen.
 
step 2
Group check
Is this one payment settling several invoices at once? If so, picking a single best match would be wrong.
 
step 3
Rank
Score the remaining candidates on amount, date and account agreement. A trained model, not a rule.
 
step 4
Decide
Post it, or hand it to a person. Bigger amounts must clear a higher bar before they post unattended.
 

No language model runs anywhere in these four steps. It is used only to write explanations afterwards, where a wrong word costs nothing and a wrong match costs money.

3Try it

Runs on records the model has never seen, and whose correct answers are known — so the accuracy below is measured against them, not asserted.

The hard case. One bank credit, several payments behind it. The system gets the amount and a pool of about a hundred candidate payments, and has to work out which ones — it is never told the batch number.

Two CSV files: what the bank says arrived, and what your books say it should be. Column names do not need to match ours — it looks for an amount, a date and an account under whatever they are called.
